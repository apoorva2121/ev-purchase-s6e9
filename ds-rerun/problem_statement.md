Needs ML at all? **Yes, but the honest answer is "yes, and the ceiling is low."** The target is not derivable by a two-line rule: no single feature separates the classes (to be quantified at Stage 3). But this is synthetic data generated by a stochastic rule, which means there is an irreducible noise ceiling well below AUC 1.0. The interesting question at Stage 7 is not "can ML learn this" but "how close to the generator's own ceiling can we get, and is the remaining gap real signal or fold noise". That framing is what makes the noise floor (Stage 2) the single most important number in this project.

## Open ASSUMED lines (carry into the report's limitations)

1. **Target observation window** — undocumented. No date column exists, so no censoring check is possible.
2. **Prediction moment** — assumed to be at profiling time, before purchase. Tested at Stage 3 via the leak screen; the assumption is recorded, not silently adopted.
3. **Decision fed / cost of errors** — assumed none. If a real cost matrix existed, the threshold work at Stage 9 would change.
4. **Feature record timing** — no data dictionary exists. Every column's `recorded_when` will be `undocumented` at Stage 3; the verdict must then rest on the column's semantics plus its univariate score, and that reasoning gets written down per column.
5. **Relationship between the 10,000-row source table and the competition data** — assumed to be "Kaggle synthesised the competition rows from this table". Measured at Stage 2 (near-duplicate structure), not assumed.
6. **Independence of rows** — assumed false until measured. 668,665 rows from a 10,000-row ancestor cannot be independent draws. Stage 2 measures the duplicate structure and Apoorva decides the split rule.
